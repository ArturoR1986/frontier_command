## Differences that matter to the build

| Decision | RimWorld emphasis in the reviewed material | Classic StarCraft emphasis | Frontier Command implementation direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What the player builds | A place where particular people work, eat, recover and develop | An economy and production network capable of winning engagements | Begin with freely arranged shelter, stockyards, food, care and workshops. Local geography and walking cost matter throughout progression. |
| Who performs work | Named people with priorities and lasting skill differences | Repeatable workers feeding an army economy | Persistent specialists execute autonomous jobs. Drafting and crewing remove them from domestic labor. |
| How resources feel | Physical items and location, production bills, delayed crops | Focused mining, supply and spending on queued production | Physical gathering/delivery with inspectable shortages. Industrial queues arrive after the colony establishes research, parts and power. |
| What experience means | A reason to specialize and protect a particular person | Tactical learning belongs mainly to the player; armies are reproducible | Practice persists. Better operators improve valuable equipment, without making a robot need a personal biography. |
| What fighting asks | Prepare defenses, draft people, treat consequences | Read the battlefield, scout, position roles, reinforce or retreat | Responsive group orders, firing lanes, range and role counters. Robots screen crewed vehicles; recovery preserves specialists and hull investment. |
| Why expand | Additional opportunity beyond a functioning home | New resources sustain strategic options and production | A large home remains useful. World routes add resource access, trade, supply, occupation and eventually realm interaction. |

## The synthesis to implement

The player builds a durable society capable of projecting force. The opening promise is to make a productive home in a terrain-rich map, not to hurry into a raid. Domestic improvement provides choices before military automation: shorten hauling, choose fertile ground, improve sleeping space, cook reliable meals, care for injuries, specialize workers and build research capacity.

Later, parts, fuel, power and research make transport and robot fabrication possible. An expedition competes with further home investment. Sending a practiced engineer to operate a carrier removes that engineer from construction. A robot loss requires fabrication inputs and time; a disabled vehicle creates a separate crew-rescue and hull-repair problem. Range, terrain, scouting and screening give military commands consequences beyond comparing hit-point totals.

The globe does not replace the home. Travel takes distance-dependent time, with vehicles changing speed, cargo and route options. Territorial control produces opportunity and supply obligations. Persistent multiplayer, offline policy and cross-realm ownership are original design problems; neither reference footage validates them. Prototype rules must be disclosed and tested, and human-play gates remain unverified until actual play is observed.

Construction/skill/research durations are initial tuning values, not claims of a validated multi-hour progression. Accelerated engineering fixtures will be labeled explicitly. The next work is implementation and review of this loop, not another general research round.
